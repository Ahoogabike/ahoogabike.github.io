Move a repair through the board

Moving a task between kanban columns is what communicates with the customer. Know what each move sends before you drag the card.

1. The kanban stages and the automatic e-mails

StageWhat it meansAutomatic e-mail
Bike in / To doBike received, work not startedCustomer receives: "We received your bike for repair/maintenance. Our shop will inform you by e-mail once the repair/maintenance has been finished."
Missing componentBlocked waiting for a partThe supply team is notified — but the e-mail only says the task is blocked; it does not say which part is missing. Add the part in a log note.
Task blockedBlocked for another reason (child not present for a kid's bike, customer forgot to leave the key, wheel not available…)—
Warranty assessmentBeing assessed as a warranty case—
Test ride before deliveryReady, to be road-tested—
Waiting for customer to pick upFinishedCustomer receives: repair done, come and collect it (with opening hours), plus the 7-day pick-up policy
Finished ticketsDelivered and paid—
Loan bike to doAn Ahooga-owned loan bike needing maintenance—
RuleAfter a repair, the bike should be ridden and tested before it is handed back. In practice the Test ride before delivery column is often skipped and the card goes straight to Waiting for customer to pick up. Do the test ride even if you skip the column.
Missing componentWhen you move a card to Missing component, always add a log note naming the exact part, and tag the supply colleague. Otherwise nobody knows what to order and the bike waits.

2. The 7-day pick-up policy

The "ready" e-mail states that, due to lack of space, Ahooga cannot keep a bike in the store for more than 7 days, and asks the customer to inform the shop by e-mail if they cannot collect in time.

Why this needs attentionThe new store is smaller and the space problem gets worse. Review bikes in Waiting for customer to pick up older than 7 days every week and chase them. How the deadline is enforced is a policy for the shop manager to define and communicate.

3. Recording what you did (and telling the customer)

1
Write what was actually done in the task description — e.g. "Basic maintenance. Found an issue on the derailleur: damaged, replaced. Punctured tyre: replaced tyre and inner tube."
WhyCustomers ask "what did you do to my bike?" — often at the till, often weeks later. Having it written down answers the question instantly and defends the invoice.
2
Note that the customer does not see the task description. They only see what you send them via Send message and the automatic stage e-mails.
3
Sending the customer a short summary of the work done is an agreed improvement worth making — it also saves repeating it at the counter.

4. Weekly board hygiene

  • Every card in Missing component has a log note naming the part, and something is actually on order.
  • Every card in Task blocked has a reason and a next action.
  • Nothing sits in Waiting for customer to pick up longer than 7 days without a chase.
  • Finished and paid repairs are moved to Finished tickets, so the board shows reality.
