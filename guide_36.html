Do a physical inventory count

Every three months. Read this before you start counting — half the value is in knowing what to count and what a strange line means.

1. The procedure

Inventory → Operations → Physical Inventory
1
Filter by Location → Search more… → Louise Stock. You get every product at that location (around 472 lines at the time of the training).
2
For each line, enter the Counted Quantity.
3
Apply — Odoo writes the adjustment.

2. Read the list before you start

Negative quantities are impossible in realityA negative line means something was sold that the system did not have in stock. It is already an error before you count anything — usually a missing receipt. Note it and find the cause.

3. Before you count, remember

  • Count the parts already fitted to repaired bikes awaiting collection. They are still on your stock list, because repair components are only deducted at collection.
  • Bikes reserved for open orders are still physically present — reserved is not gone.
  • Stock can also sit at locations you cannot sell from (for example bikes away at an event). Count the location you filtered, not the building.

4. The five structural causes of wrong stock

Understand these and you can prevent most of the damage.

1
Repairs deplete stock only at collection. Parts fitted but not yet collected are still counted as stock.
2
Warranty deliveries are never validated. The bike goes out free and nobody validates the transfer. More than 170 open transfers existed at one point.
3
Leasing / B2B orders handled incorrectly — wrong invoicing route, transfers left unvalidated.
4
Bad inbound. Goods arrive and are not received in Odoo, or are taken from the warehouse without a transfer. Both sides of the move end up wrong.
5
The Modular's assembly structure. Worth reading twice — see below.

The Modular case

A Modular is delivered by the frame manufacturer without battery, pedals, saddle, display, charger, racks, kickstand or child-seat bags. Ahooga assembles those in its own warehouse, so Odoo has to deplete stock at several separate steps. The consequence: a Modular sales order with 3 lines generates a delivery with 14 lines.

When you stand in front of a Modular during an inventory count and register "one Modular, serial 23456", you have not registered the kickstand, the front rack, the rear rack, the battery, the charger, the pedals, the saddle, the seat post, the lights and the bell. The stock of those 14 components at Louise is therefore permanently wrong.

This is a legacy of how supply was set up, and it disappears with the Modular: the model is being discontinued, is not moving to the new store, and is being sold off. Until then, count Modulars component by component.

5. After the count

6
Log what you adjusted and why — the next person needs to know whether a figure was counted or corrected.
7
Follow up the negative lines and the missing receipts you found. The count is a diagnosis, not a cure.
8
Archive obsolete products while you are in there — the catalogue holds roughly 700+ products and a smaller catalogue is a more accurate catalogue.
Open question — scanningWhether the count can be done with barcode scanning rather than by hand is unresolved. A stronger idea discussed: RFID tags, where an antenna reads the whole store in minutes and produces a discrepancy list. In a large retail environment this reduced a daily two-hour count to 10–30 minutes. Worth investigating with IT for both the shop and the warehouse; the investment is in the tags.
