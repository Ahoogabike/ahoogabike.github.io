Stock coverage
Cover = (on hand + on order − customer backlog) ÷ forecast monthly demand for 2027. Overstock means you are holding more than the threshold above; short means less than the lead time allows you to replace. Demand is after substitution, so a SKU losing share to a new one shows its coverage getting worse without anyone touching the stock.
Demand pools & substitution
Accessories that do the same job compete for the same customer. Each pool gets one attach rate — the share of riders who buy something from it — and the members split that demand between them. Shares are weights: they are normalised to 100%, so pushing one up automatically pulls the others down. That is what stops the plan from buying a rear bag and a front bag for the same rider.
Standalone accessories
No substitute — attach rate measured from 2026 shipments, excluding dealer starter sets. Override if you expect it to move.
Demand build 2027
Rider demand = attach × bikes reaching a rider. Starter sets go to new doors, retrofit is a one-off for the existing dealer base on the new SKUs only.
Monthly, to December 2027
Rider demand follows the accessory season curve measured from your own history — it peaks later than bike shipments because it follows dealer sell-through. Starter sets and the retrofit land in Q1.
When to order
Rolls stock forward month by month and raises an order when cover would fall below the safety level, sized to carry the next six months less anything already on its way. Nothing is allowed to arrive sooner than the lead time from today.
