Stock discipline and stale transfers

Ten rules to put on the wall, and the monthly clean-up that keeps the shop's stock honest.
Stock accuracy is the single biggest structural problem in the shop, and the cost is mainly time: looking for parts that are not there, and re-committing to customers on the basis of wrong figures.

1. The stock-discipline checklist

Print this and put it on the wall.

1
Nothing enters the shop without a validated receipt.
2
Nothing leaves the shop without a validated transfer — including warranty and loan bikes.
3
Customer collects in the shop → POS. Goods are shipped → back office. Never both.
4
If an item on an order is unavailable, remove the line in the POS; do not validate in the back office instead.
5
Never sell an accessory you do not physically have.
6
Always select an existing product; never create one by pressing Enter on free text.
7
Set Warehouse = Louise and Sales Team = Point of Sale Louise on every shop order.
8
Review the red pickings in the POS sessions weekly.
9
Review Waiting / Ready transfers with source = Louise Stock monthly, and close or cancel the stale ones.
10
Count every three months, including parts fitted to bikes awaiting collection.

2. Hunting stale transfers

Inventory → Transfers / Deliveries → filter status Waiting + Ready → filter Source location = Louise Stock

You will find transfers years old. For each one, open the sales order and decide:

The customer did take the goodsValidate the transfer — the goods really left.
The customer never took the goodsSet the sales-order line quantity to 0, which cancels the transfer.
It is a duplicate of a POS pickingCancel it.
11
Then log a note saying what you decided. This list is your stock clean-up backlog.
Make it a saved searchSave this filter (Favourites → Save current search, tick Shared) so the list is one click away every month.

3. Where the errors come from

If the same problem keeps coming back, the cause is usually one of the five structural ones listed in Do a physical inventory count: repairs depleting only at collection, unvalidated warranty deliveries, mishandled leasing orders, bad inbound, and the Modular's assembly structure.

Not only a shop problemWarehouse stock has its own accuracy problems, and fixing warehouse management is the stated reason for moving to an external warehouse. Do not assume a figure is right because it comes from the warehouse.
