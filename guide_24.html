Settle an order and hand over at the till

The customer is in front of you to collect a bike or a repair. This is the sequence, including discounts, split payments and what to do when something on the order is missing.

1. Find and settle the order

1
Point of Sale → Continue selling (or open the session).
2
Actions → Quotation/Order.
3
Find the order — scroll the list, or type the order number in the search bar.
4
Settle the order. The order lines are loaded into the till.

2. Serial numbers

If you assigned a serial number to the bike on the delivery in the back office, the POS asks whether you want to load the serial numbers linked to the sales order. Say yes. The line then shows the serial number and turns green.

Red lineIf the line is red, no bike is matched to the order: the serial number has not been assigned, or you do not have that bike in stock. Fix that before taking the money.
RuleBefore the customer arrives, assign the physical bike to the order and put a paper on the bike with the customer name and order number. Then this step takes seconds.

3. Changing the order at the till

You can modify everything in the POS before payment:

  • Add a product the customer decides they want (a cushion, a helmet…). Ask — this is where accessory sales are won.
  • Remove a line, or set its quantity to 0, if they changed their mind — or if you do not have the item (section 5).
  • Change quantity: click Quantity and type the new number.

4. Discounts — do it the traceable way

RuleGive a discount by adding the "Commercial discount" product as a line with a negative amount (quantity 1, price −25 for a €25 discount). Do not use the global percentage-discount action in the POS Actions menu.
WhyBecause the discount is a product, every discount is recorded, reportable and reviewable: you can see who discounted how much, on what. The global Actions → Discount → 100% route applies a blanket percentage to the whole order and leaves nothing analysable behind.

5. When an item on the order is not available

This is the exact situation that produces most of the shop's stock errors: a customer collects a bike, but one or two accessories on the order are not in stock, so the whole delivery cannot be validated through the POS.

Correct handlingIn the POS, after Settle the order, remove the unavailable line(s) — or set the quantity to what you are actually handing over. The POS then delivers exactly what left the shop, the customer pays exactly for what they received, and the remaining quantity stays open on the sales order.
5
Then decide, with a log note on the order, what happens to the rest: the customer will come back for it, or the line should be set to 0 and the order closed.
Write the decision downAn order left ambiguous becomes a phantom transfer that someone will be investigating in a year's time.

6. Taking the payment

6
Click Payment.
7
Click "Invoice". If you forget this, no invoice is generated for the customer.
8
Choose the payment method. Bank covers card payments; Cash for cash. If the customer already paid by bank transfer in advance, choose Bank transfer.
9
Split payments are allowed: click Cash, type 1000, then click Bank — Bank takes the remainder automatically.
10
Validate. The receipt screen appears — this is what shows on the customer-facing screen in the shop.
Down paymentsA down payment already taken appears as a negative line, so the till only asks for the balance.

7. Before you walk away

  • The invoice was requested at payment.
  • Every line handed over is on the ticket; every line not handed over was removed or set to 0.
  • A log note records what happens to anything left open.
  • You did not also validate the delivery in the back office (see POS vs back office).
