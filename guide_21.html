Take a down payment

Two routes — back office for a customer who is not in the shop, POS for a customer who is. They end up in different places, so pick the right one.
Why we ask for oneTo prepare a bike, Ahooga wants a payment. The guideline is a minimum of about 20%; historically the shop asked a fixed €1.000.

Route A — down payment invoice from the back office

Use this when the customer is not in the shop and will pay by bank transfer.

1
On the confirmed sales order: Create Invoice.
2
Choose Down payment (fixed amount) — or a percentage — and enter the amount.
3
Create Draft Invoice. A new invoice icon appears on the order.
4
Open the draft and check: invoice address, delivery address, invoice date, due date, payment terms (immediate, 7 days…).
5
Confirm — the invoice gets its number.
6
Send it: by e-mail, by Peppol (for B2B e-invoicing), by post, or Download. For B2C, e-mail is enough; for B2B, send by Peppol as well.
7
When the customer sends proof of payment, you can schedule the delivery.

Route B — down payment through the POS

Use this when the customer is in the shop and pays now.

Point of Sale → open your session → Actions → Quotation/Order → find the order → Apply a down payment → enter the amount → Payment → Invoice → choose Bank/Cash → Validate

The down payment is registered on the order, and you will see it later as a negative line when you settle the balance at the till.

Checking that a payment actually arrived

Shop staff do not have access to the bank account. Two options:

8
Look at the invoice: the Amount due drops and a Paid banner appears once Finance has reconciled the payment against the invoice.
9
Or log a note on the order tagging the finance colleague, asking them to confirm when it is paid.
WarningUse Log note, not Send message — otherwise you e-mail your internal question to the customer.
