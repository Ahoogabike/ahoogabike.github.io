Odoo essentials for the shop team

The handful of basics that every other shop procedure depends on: the two databases, the apps you use, how to navigate, and the chatter.
Read this firstAlmost every mistake made in Odoo comes from not knowing the four or five things on this page. Learn them once and the rest of the system becomes predictable.
On this page
1. The two Odoo environments 2. The applications you will use 3. Navigating: everything is connected 4. Search, filters and saved searches 5. Views and pagination 6. The chatter: message, log note, activity 7. Colour codes you must be able to read

1. The two Odoo environments

Ahooga runs two Odoo databases. Always know which one you are in.

EnvironmentWhat it isUse it for
ProductionThe live company database. Real stock movements, real invoices, real e-mails to real customers.All actual work.
TestA copy of production, refreshed periodically. Same data, same layout, no consequences.Learning, practising, testing a process before you use it live.
Check the URL before you click anythingThe two environments look identical. Bookmark both links separately and label the bookmarks clearly. When in doubt, look at the browser address bar first.
RuleAnything you have never done before, you do in test first — creating a quotation, settling an order at the till, running a physical inventory. Do it once in test, then do it for real.
Access rightsRights are granted per environment. Having access in production does not give you access in test, and the other way round. If an application is missing from your Odoo home screen, that is an access-rights question for the Head of Sales — not a bug.

3. Navigating: everything is connected

Every Odoo application is linked to the others, and you can move between them from inside a record.

Contacts → open the customer → click the "Sales" smart button → you are in Sales, filtered to that customer's orders

On a contact record, smart buttons summarise that customer's whole history:

  • Sales — how many sales orders they have
  • Point of Sale orders — how many times they have paid at the till
  • Invoiced — total invoiced value
  • Tasks — how many repairs they have had
Why this mattersYou almost never need to search twice. If you have the customer, you have their repairs, their orders and their invoices one click away — exactly what you need when someone walks in and says "I was here in March".

The breadcrumb trail

Top-left of every screen, Odoo shows the path you took (for example Contacts / Customer name / Sales / S026163). Each element is clickable, so you step back one level at a time instead of restarting your search. Use it constantly.

4. Search, filters and saved searches

Click the dropdown arrow in the search bar of any list and you always get the same three columns:

1
Filters — narrow the records (status, date, team, country…).
2
Group By — cluster the records (by salesperson, by product category, by warehouse…).
3
Favourites — saved searches.

Combine filters and group-bys freely, then save the result: Favourites → Save current search, give it a name, tick Shared if colleagues should see it too. From then on it is one click away.

Saved searches worth building on day one

  • Sales orders where Sales Team = Point of Sale Louise, created this year
  • Leasing orders: Customer is in (your leasing partners)
  • Deliveries Waiting / Ready with Source location = Louise Stock — your stock-error hunting list

"My" filters

When you log in with your own user, most lists open pre-filtered to your records (for example My Quotations in Sales). To see more, remove the filter; to see the shop, add Sales Team = Point of Sale Louise.

5. Views and pagination

ListThe workhorse. Click the small gear/columns icon at the right of the header row to add columns: delivery date, expected date, taxes, tags, invoice status, warehouse.
KanbanCards in columns. This is how repairs and quotation stages are managed.
CalendarScheduled activities and expected delivery dates. Filter first (e.g. Sales Team = Point of Sale Louise), otherwise you see the whole company.
PivotCross-tabulate: sales orders per month, per product category.
GraphThe same data as a chart.
Pagination trickOdoo shows a page of records at a time (e.g. 1-80 / 273). Click the counter and type a bigger range (1-273) to see everything on one page. Essential when hunting for a serial number or a stray transfer.

6. The chatter: message, log note, activity

At the bottom of nearly every record there are three buttons. The difference between the first two is the single most dangerous confusion in Odoo.

ButtonWho sees itUse it for
Send messageThe customer (and followers)Anything you want the customer to read.
Log noteInternal only (and followers)Internal remarks, questions to colleagues, explanations of what you did and why.
Schedule activityYou / the assigneeA to-do with a date: "call this customer tomorrow".
WarningSend message e-mails the customer. If you meant to write an internal remark and you click Send message, the customer receives it. Always check which tab is active before you type.

Tagging colleagues

In a log note, type @ and the person's name to notify them. Typical uses: the finance colleague for "please tell me when this sales order is paid"; the supply colleague for lead times and part availability; the Head of Sales for system, process and routing questions.

Why notifications get ignoredAnyone you tag becomes a follower of that record and then receives an e-mail for every later change. Long follower lists produce notifications that are not for the reader, and those notifications stop being read. Keep follower lists short and be deliberate about who you tag — you can remove followers from the followers list.

Activities

A scheduled activity appears as a coloured button on the record and in the Calendar view. If it turns red, it is overdue. Activities are currently little used in the shop — using them is a cheap way to stop losing quotations.

7. Colour codes you must be able to read

Product availability on a line

  • Blue / green — available, and reserved for this order.
  • Red — not available: either you have none, or what you have is already reserved for another order, or no serial number has been assigned yet.
RuleNever validate a delivery or promise a date on a red line without checking why it is red.

The e-mail status icon

  • Normal / grey — the e-mail was sent.
  • Red — the e-mail did not reach the recipient. Almost always because the e-mail address on the contact is wrong. Fix the contact, then resend.
