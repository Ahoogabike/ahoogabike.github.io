Add parts and labour, hand back the bike, take the money

The billing half of a repair — and the one stock behaviour that catches everybody out.

1. Add the parts and the labour

At the top of the repair task, open the products / order section and add:

1
Every part used — pick the exact product. Note that families share components: the Modular and Urban share the same frame and almost all components, so parts are often listed once for both.
2
Labour — add the labour/maintenance service line for the time spent.

As soon as you add products, Odoo creates a confirmed sales order on the task, and a smart button appears with the order number. That number is what you use at the till.

Never skip the labour lineParts without labour means giving the work away. If the job took time, the time is a line.

2. The repair stock trap

WarningThe components you fit to the bike are not deducted from stock when you fit them. They are deducted when the delivery is done — that is, when the customer collects the bike and you settle the order.

So a bike sitting in Waiting for customer to pick up has a new derailleur fitted to it while Odoo still counts that derailleur on your shelf.
Consequence for inventory countsWhen you do an inventory count, you must also count the parts already fitted to repaired bikes that have not yet been collected — otherwise you will "find" a shortage that is not real, correct it, and make the stock worse.
Open pointWhether the repair app can deplete components on validation of the repair (operation type Louise repairs) instead of on collection was raised but not confirmed. Confirm this before relying on either behaviour.

3. Hand the bike back and take the money

Point of Sale → Open register → Actions → Quotation/Order → enter the repair's sales order number → Settle the order → Payment → Invoice → Bank → Validate

Exactly the same as a bike delivery. The repair's sales order behaves like any other sales order — including the rule that a collection goes through the POS and never also through the back office. See Settle an order and hand over at the till.

3
Before handing over, do the test ride if it has not been done.
4
Tell the customer what was done — the task description is your script.
5
Move the task to Finished tickets.

4. Prepayments and phone quotes

  • A customer asks for a repair quote by phone. Customer service gives a price (parts + inner tube + 30 minutes labour). Make sure that price ends up written on the task, so the shop and the office quote the same number.
  • A customer pays in advance by bank transfer for a repair. The bike is not repaired yet, but the money has arrived. Treat it as a normal POS case at collection: settle the order and click Bank transfer as the payment method, since the transfer has already been made. Do not invent a new procedure for it.

5. Loan bikes

  • Ahooga has roughly 8–10 loan bikes.
  • They are currently given free of charge, typically when a repair cannot be done quickly (for example a motor to replace with a part missing for a week) and the customer cannot be without a bike.
  • Loan bikes need maintenance themselves, and that work goes through the Loan bike to do stage in the same project.
To confirmThere is no written rule yet on who gets a loan bike, for how long, or whether a maintenance-only customer can have one. Until there is, check with the shop manager before promising one. See Open points and decisions.
