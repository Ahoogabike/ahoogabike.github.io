Create and maintain a customer contact

A contact is not an address book entry. In Odoo the contact record drives automatic behaviour — VAT, e-mail language, and whether the customer hears from you at all.

1. Why contact data quality decides everything downstream

CountryDetermines the fiscal position, which determines the VAT rate applied to every order for that customer. Belgium → 21%. France → 20%. No country → Odoo does not know what to do.
LanguageDetermines the language of every automatic e-mail Odoo sends that customer (quotation, repair received, repair ready, invoice). No language set → the customer gets the wrong language.
E-mail addressDetermines whether the customer receives anything at all. A wrong e-mail is the single most common cause of "I never heard from you" complaints.
RuleFill in as much as you can, always: name, street, zip, city, country, phone, mobile, e-mail, language. Two minutes here saves an hour later.

2. Creating a contact — two ways

A. From the Contacts app

Contacts → New
1
Choose Individual or Company.
2
Fill in the fields below and save.

B. On the fly from a quotation — what you will do 90% of the time

3
In a new quotation, start typing the customer name in the Customer field.
4
If the contact exists, Odoo proposes it — select it.
5
If not, Odoo offers Create "name" and Create and edit…. Choose Create and edit…; the contact form opens in a pop-up, you fill it in, Save & Close, and you are back in the quotation with the customer set.
Warning — duplicatesAlways type a few letters and look at the proposals before creating. Duplicate contacts split a customer's history in two, so you no longer see their real purchase and repair record. Duplicates are already a known problem in the database — do not add to it.

3. The fields on a contact

Header / main block

  • Individual or Company
  • Name, and for an individual the company they belong to, if relevant
  • Address: street, zip, city, country
  • Phone and Mobile — once the country is Belgium, a locally typed number is understood and normalised to +32
  • WhatsApp — mainly used for B2B and dealers, rarely for shop customers
  • E-mail
  • Language
  • Tags

Tab: Contact & Addresses

Child contacts. This is how B2B customers are structured: one parent company with many child records for delivery addresses and contact people.

ExampleA dealer with several shops is one company record containing a child address for each shop. When you create a sales order you select the company as the customer and then pick the correct Delivery address child record — otherwise the goods go to the wrong shop.

Tab: Sales & Purchase

  • Salesperson responsible for this customer
  • Payment terms (immediate payment, 30 days…)
  • Payment method (optional)
  • Price list — each price list is a different margin level. B2C shop customers use the Public Pricelist.
  • Delivery method
  • A separate block of vendor fields, used when the contact is a supplier rather than a customer

Other tabs

  • Accounting — bank accounts and accounting defaults.
  • Internal Notes — free text, internal only.
  • Partner Assignment — mainly B2B / dealer network use.

Fiscal position sits on the Sales & Purchase / Accounting side. If you want all orders for this customer to use one fiscal regime, set it here.

4. Fiscal positions in practice

SituationFiscal positionVAT
Sale in Belgium, B2C or B2BRégime national / Regime EU privé21%
B2C customer in another EU countryOSS B2CLocal VAT rate of the customer's country (Ahooga pays it in that country)
B2B company outside Belgium with a valid VAT numberIntra-community / export regime0%
WhyThe fiscal position is what makes VAT correct automatically. If you leave it empty on a foreign contact you will either overcharge the customer or create an accounting correction for the finance colleague later.

5. What a contact tells you at a glance

Open any customer and read the smart buttons: how many sales, how many POS tickets, total invoiced, how many repair tasks. Before you answer any customer question, look at this. It answers most of them.
